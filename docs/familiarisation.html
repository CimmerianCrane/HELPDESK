The shape of it

01

The crane is the centre of the data model. Every job, report, certificate, part movement, schedule and reminder hangs off one. A crane belongs to one site, and a site belongs to one customer. That single decision explains most of what you will see in the schema and on the screens.

Customeraccount, terms, UEN
Siteaddress, access notes, coordinates
The craneasset tag · type · SWL · registration · status
Jobs & reportsthe work and its signed paperwork
Schedulesrecurring obligations, next due
Certificatesthe AE's paper and its expiry
Findingsdefects still open on it
Stock movementswhich part, which serial, which job
Contractsagreed SLA targets and renewal

The crane page is therefore the screen to learn first. From it you reach the timeline of every report on the machine, its obligations and next-due dates, its certificates, the findings still open, the parts fitted, and the printable QR label that opens the record from a phone camera.

Two consequences worth knowing early. A job must have a site, because somebody has to drive there; a ticket need not. And a crane is never deleted, only decommissioned, because reports and certificates cite it forever.

The path work takes

02
Ticket→ Triage→ Quote→ Job→ Schedule→ Dispatch→ On the phone→ Report approved→ Invoice→ Closed

A call arrives as a ticket and is triaged, which sets the priority and starts the response clock. One ticket can become several jobs, and a job covers one or more cranes. Jobs move through a status machine: every legal transition lives in one map in the code, and a status absent from it is refused rather than stored.

Statuses: New · Triaged · Quoted · Approved · Scheduled · Dispatched · In progress · On hold · Completed · Reviewed · Invoiced · Closed · Cancelled.

01
Priority and SLA.

P1 means crane down or someone at risk, and alerts every dispatcher immediately by in-app row, email, SMS and WhatsApp. Response and resolution deadlines are snapshotted onto the job at creation, so a later policy edit cannot rewrite history.

02
On hold always has a reason.

Awaiting parts, customer access, quote approval, the AE, weather, or other. Enforced by a Zod rule, a service rule and a Postgres CHECK constraint. An awaiting-parts hold can name the catalogue part it is waiting for.

03
The field report is filled on the phone.

Template-driven sections, the checklist with measurements, photos compressed on the device, parts issued as stock movements, findings, and both signatures, or a recorded reason the customer did not sign. It works with no signal.

04
Approval is what makes it a document.

Approving a report assigns its number, renders the PDF with signatures and photographs embedded, locks it, and moves the job Completed → Reviewed. A correction is an amendment linked to the original, never an edit.

05
Money last, and only from a reviewed job.

A quote is optional and moves the job Quoted → Approved when accepted. An invoice pre-fills from the parts actually issued, snapshots the GST rate at issue, and moves the job to Invoiced.

Five rules that hold the code together

05

Read these before writing a slice. Almost every file follows one of them, and a change that breaks one is a bug even when the tests pass.

1. Authorization has two halves

permissions.ts decides whether a role may act; scope.ts decides which rows. Every read spreads its filter into the where. A capability without a scope filter is a bug, not a shortcut.

export async function listJobs(context, filters, client = db) {
  assertCan(context, 'job.read.all')
  return client.job.findMany({
    where: { ...jobReadWhere(context), ...validated(filters) },
  })
}

2. Every business write is audited

withAudit() runs the mutation and its audit row in one transaction, recording only the fields that changed. A new entity should get its xxxReadWhere in scope.ts before its first service, so the fail-closed default is there from the start.

3. One function writes stock

postMovement in src/server/inventory/stock.ts is the only writer of StockMovement and the StockLevel it moves, in the same transaction. Issuing a part on a report goes through it, so the ledger and the shelf cannot disagree. Stock may go negative; a signed field report is never refused over a shelf count.

4. An issued report is immutable

The template version is stored on the report, not read from the job type at render time, so an old document always re-renders as issued. Corrections are amendments linked to the original with a reason. Nothing is hard-deleted: reports, certificates and audit rows are archived or retired.

5. Anything the clock decides is derived on read

Overdue schedules, certificate buckets, quote expiry, invoice paid status, on-hand, a serial's whereabouts, purchase order status, stock take status, renewal badges. Nothing stores a flag that a stored date could contradict, and no nightly job flips one.

Also fixed by design

Timestamps in UTC, displayed as DD/MM/YYYY. Timezone, currency and GST rate are settings, never constants. Compliance rules are Admin-editable data with a source reference and a review date; no regulatory fact is hard-coded (see docs/ASSUMPTIONS-LOG.md 97). Our reports are company inspections and never claim to be an AE's statutory certificate.

The phone, offline

06

The technician app is four pieces, and new work extends them rather than adding a fifth way of doing the same thing.

  • Read layer. src/server/tech/my-jobs.ts. Every query spreads jobReadWhere(context). Another technician's job is a 404, not a 403, because a 403 confirms it exists.
  • One sync call. GET /api/tech/sync returns the whole bundle: open jobs, cranes, checklist items, parts, site contacts and the operating timezone. One request, because a phone at a site gate is the worst place to run eight.
  • One cache. src/offline/db.ts (Dexie) writes the bundle in a single multi-table transaction, clearing the old rows first.
  • One component, two renderers. The server pages and the /offline shell render the same list and job sheet. Never fork them.
Never lose field data. The draft lives in IndexedDB, not Postgres, until it is signed. Its id is generated on the phone, so a retried sync returns the first result instead of creating a second report. A rejected report stays queued with the server's reason on screen.
The service worker is off under npm run dev. Offline behaviour is checked against npm run build && npm start. There is also no inbox tab in the phone nav yet, so a technician reaches /inbox only from a browser.

Running it

07

Node 20.11 or newer, and either a hosted Supabase project or the bundled Docker Postgres. The app lives in cimmerian-crane/; every command below runs from there.

CommandWhat it does
npm run setupInstall, migrate and seed in one go
npm run devDev server on localhost:3000
npm run dev:stopStop whatever holds the dev port (use this, not taskkill)
npm run seedDemo estate and logins; idempotent
npm run verifyTypecheck, lint and unit tests. Fast, no database
npm run test:integrationVitest against the real database
npm run test:e2ePlaywright, office and technician projects
npm run buildProduction build
npm run db:studioPrisma Studio, to look at rows directly

A slice is not done until all four gates pass: verify, test:integration, test:e2e and build.

Your first hour, in this order:

  1. Sign in as the dispatcher, open /dashboard, and follow two tiles into the lists they link to.
  2. Open /tickets and look at the two seeded P1s, then a job they became.
  3. Open a crane and read its timeline, its schedules and its certificates.
  4. Sign in as Ahmad on a phone-sized window, open /m/jobs, and tap into a job.
  5. Open /parts, filter to below reorder, then open /parts/reorder.

Traps and known gaps

09

Things that have already cost time

A running npm run dev locks the Prisma engine, so prisma generate fails until you run npm run dev:stop first. The e2e suite runs against the dev server, so editing source mid-run hot-reloads the page under a test. Every spec imports its test from tests/e2e/fixtures.ts, which clears the sign-in throttle; importing Playwright directly is how the next spec breaks.

Reads inside a transaction take its client

The pooled connection has a small limit, so a helper that opens a second connection while a transaction is open deadlocks. Services pass tx down to every helper that reads. Any new helper takes client: Tx = db.

Declared but not built

SMS_DRIVER=twilio and WHATSAPP_DRIVER=meta fall back to the console driver with a warning. No live Xero call has been made from this repository and no credentials are in it, so the first real push is the real test.

Honest limits written on the screens

Profitability costs parts only; labour is not costed, and the column says so. Utilisation assumes a fixed Monday to Friday week with no holiday calendar. Route distances are great-circle and drive time assumes 30 km/h. The SLA filter runs in memory over a 2,000-row cap.
